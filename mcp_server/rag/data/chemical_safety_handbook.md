# GreenField AgroWorks — Chemical Safety & Compliance Handbook

---

## Re-Entry Intervals (REI) and Field Safety Rules

The Re-Entry Interval (REI) is the mandatory period immediately following a chemical application during which agricultural workers are strictly prohibited from entering a treated field without specialized personal protective equipment (PPE). 

Key REI Operational Rules:
1. **Default Intervals:** Standard fertilizers and unrestricted pesticides carry a baseline 12-hour or 24-hour REI. Restricted-use chemicals (such as Herbicide X) carry a mandatory 48-hour REI.
2. **Scheduling Overrides:** An active REI overrides all routine field operations, including irrigation checks, soil testing, and weeding. No field hand may enter the field during this window.
3. **Warning Signage:** Red warning signs displaying the chemical name, date of application, and exact time of REI expiration must be posted at all primary field access points prior to spraying.
4. **Emergency Re-Entry:** In rare emergencies (e.g., sudden equipment retrieval), workers entering before REI expiration must wear full PPE, including chemical-resistant overalls, rubber boots, nitrile gloves, and full-face respirators.

## Pre-Harvest Intervals (PHI) and Buyer Audit Compliance

The Pre-Harvest Interval (PHI) is the legally mandated minimum number of days that must elapse between the last chemical application and the physical harvest of the crop. PHI rules ensure chemical residues decay to levels compliant with EPA and FDA food safety standards.

Key PHI Operational Rules:
1. **Crop-Specific Restrictions:** PHI durations vary by crop and growth stage. For example, Tomatoes require a minimum 14-day PHI after applying Restricted-Use Pesticides, while Corn requires a 21-day PHI.
2. **Harvest Blocking:** Field prep and harvesting schedules must be cross-referenced against chemical log dates. Attempting to harvest prior to PHI expiration will result in immediate crop quarantine.
3. **Buyer Audit Documentation:** Commercial buyers require verified PHI logs prior to purchasing. Every compliance report generated for buyer audits must clearly indicate the last chemical treatment date, the application rate, and the verified PHI expiration date.

## Buffer Zones and Environmental Drift Protection

Buffer zones are non-treated geographic areas surrounding a spray field designed to prevent chemical drift onto non-target crops, waterways, residential areas, or adjacent livestock pastures.

Key Buffer Zone Rules:
1. **Aquatic & Waterway Buffers:** A minimum 100-foot untreated buffer zone must be maintained around ponds, streams, drainage ditches, and irrigation wells.
2. **Property & Crop Line Buffers:** Maintain a 50-foot buffer from property boundaries and neighboring fields planted with non-target or organic crops.
3. **Wind & Weather Limits:** Spraying is strictly prohibited if wind speeds exceed 10 mph or if ambient temperatures create atmospheric inversion conditions that promote chemical drift.

## Restricted-Use Pesticide (RUP) Regulations and Applicator Certification

Restricted-Use Pesticides (RUPs) present higher environmental or acute toxicity risks and are subject to strict state and federal statutory requirements.

Key Regulatory Requirements:
1. **State Legal Requirement:** Under state law, RUPs (such as Herbicide X) can only be purchased, requested, or applied by—or under the direct supervision of—a Certified Applicator or Agronomist (e.g., Sara Ali).
2. **System Enforcement:** Uncertified field hands are legally prohibited from scheduling or approving RUP applications. System rejections of RUP requests by non-certified personnel are enforced to comply with state law, not internal corporate policy.
3. **Application Logs:** Every RUP application requires a formal pending request record, an explicit approval record signed by a certified worker, and a documented post-application evaluation.

## Emergency Spill Response and Chemical Containment Protocols

In the event of an accidental chemical leak, tank rupture, or spill during mixing or application, staff must immediately implement containment procedures.

Key Emergency Actions:
1. **Evacuate and Protect:** Evacuate non-essential personnel upwind of the spill. Ensure all response personnel immediately don chemical-resistant suits, heavy-duty nitrile gloves, and respirators.
2. **Stop and Contain:** If safe to do so, shut off valves or right overturned containers. Prevent liquid runoff from reaching irrigation canals, soil drainage paths, or water sources by constructing earth berms or applying absorbent spill kits.
3. **Notification & Reporting:** Immediately notify the Farm Safety Director and call the state spill reporting hotlines for any RUP spill exceeding 1 gallon of concentrate or 10 gallons of diluted spray mixture.
